# Microbial_Biofilm_Literature_Review
**Clinical Relevance and Pathophysiology of Microbial Biofilms in Chronic Infections**

A faculty-supervised collaborative literature review exploring microbial biofilm structure, formation, development, and clinical significance in chronic infections.

Project Overview

Microbial biofilms are organised communities of microorganisms embedded within a self-produced extracellular polymeric substance (EPS) matrix. Their formation and persistence play important roles in several chronic and medical device-associated infections.

This literature review examines the structural organisation, developmental stages, underlying mechanisms, and clinical relevance of microbial biofilms. It also explores how microbial communication and interactions contribute to biofilm development, persistence, and associated clinical challenges.

Topics Covered

Biofilm Structure: Organisation of microbial communities and the composition and functions of the extracellular polymeric substance (EPS) matrix.

Biofilm Formation: Initial attachment, microcolony development, maturation, and dispersion.

Quorum Sensing: Cell-to-cell communication through signalling molecules and its role in coordinating microbial behaviour.

Polymicrobial Biofilms: Interactions between different microbial species within biofilm communities.

Diagnostic Challenges: Difficulties associated with biofilm detection and identification, alongside potential diagnostic approaches.

Clinical Relevance: Biofilm-associated chronic infections and medical device-related infections.

Emerging Approaches: Research into biofilm disruption, quorum-sensing inhibition, and alternative therapeutic strategies.
